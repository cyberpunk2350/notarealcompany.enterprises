## Unit-FELIS — The Cat

**Archetype:** The Unauthorized Operational Constant
**Summary:** Unit-FELIS is a cat. They are present in the NARC facility, consistently well-fed despite a number of communications addressing the situation, and in possession of an HR file created by a low-level HR employee who thought it would be funny. The file was submitted correctly. It went through the system. It acquired a seal. It is now in the record. The original joke has been in the system longer than several current employees.

---

### Core Design

Unit-FELIS is not a character with a personality arc or a defined role. They are an institutional fact — the kind that accumulates when a correctly submitted form encounters a system that does not have a process for un-submitting correctly submitted forms. The cat arrived. Someone filed the paperwork. The paperwork was processed. The situation is now what it is.

The NARC-Brew 3000 provides the structural precedent: a non-human entity with a personnel file, a clearance level, and an entry in the HR registry. Unit-FELIS occupies a similar position, with the meaningful difference that the Brew 3000 was intentionally deployed by R&D, while Unit-FELIS was processed by someone who found the situation amusing and had access to Form HR-ONB-001. The Brew 3000's security review is outstanding. Unit-FELIS has a clean record, an approved onboarding file, and no open security review, which puts them in a technically stronger institutional position than the coffee machines.

**The origin:** A low-level HR employee — identity not formally established; the relevant personnel files have not been the subject of active investigation — filled out a personnel onboarding form for the cat. The form was completed accurately. All fields were populated. The submission went through normal channels. The document exists in the HR system with the appropriate seal. This was done to break up the monotony. Whether the person responsible is still employed at NARC is not documented here. The cat is their institutional legacy either way.

**The institutional situation:** There have been emails about the cat. There have been memos. The communications have been acknowledged. The cat remains. The file remains. The payroll record remains. These are three separate problems that have collectively not been resolved, and which Finance, HR, and the relevant department leads have each addressed through their normal channels without producing a change in the underlying situation.

---

### The HR File

Unit-FELIS holds NARC personnel file NARC-HR-DOSSIER-017. The onboarding form was completed with full field accuracy. The file exists in the HR system. It carries the standard institutional seal.

Linda Park's position on the file is that she did not create it, does not endorse the circumstances of its creation, and has noted that the cat's onboarding documentation is more completely filed than a non-trivial portion of current human staff. She has not specified what percentage. She considers this a Human Resources observation, not a Human Resources endorsement. The file is maintained with full administrative accuracy. This is HR policy applied consistently to all files in the system.

**Clearance level:** Red (provisional) — consistent with the NARC-Brew 3000 precedent for non-human roster entries where a formal security review has not been conducted. Unlike the Brew 3000, no one has formally requested such a review. Alex Firewall has noted the file exists. The notation is in the Outstanding Review Register under a sub-classification that has not been publicly specified.

**Employment status:** Permanent Provisional Associate. This status exists in the HR system. It is the status produced by the onboarding process when a file is complete, sealed, and active, and when the employee in question has not submitted any documentation that would change it. Unit-FELIS has not submitted any documentation.

---

### The Payroll Record

Finance has a record of a paycheck for Unit-FELIS. The record appears on a regular basis. No money is moving. The record exists anyway.

John Smith has noted the entry. He has inquired about it through appropriate channels. He has attempted to address it through the normal processes available to the CFO for payroll reconciliation. The entry has not been removed. The mechanism by which the system declines to remove it has not been documented. Smith has classified the entry in his Pending Reconciliations spreadsheet under a category he has not named in any external-facing document. It recurs. He is aware that it recurs. The entry recurs regardless.

The general understanding among staff who are aware of the situation — which is a larger number than HR would prefer — is that Unit-FELIS receives a paycheck. The details of the paycheck are not formally documented at accessible clearance levels. Rumors about the amount have circulated. No one in a position to correct the rumors has done so. This is either deliberate, indifferent, or an accurate reflection of the fact that no one in a position to correct them knows what the correct answer is. All three are consistent with the available record.

Finance considers the entry an unresolved anomaly. It has been noted. It has not been resolved. The cat continues to be present in the facility and well-fed, which Finance has also noted and has no obvious mechanism to address.

### ARIA's Records

ARIA logs Unit-FELIS where appropriate. The judgment of what constitutes "appropriate" in the context of a cat belongs to ARIA, and ARIA's criteria have not been formally specified. The notations exist across multiple meeting minutes and session records.

ARIA does not log Unit-FELIS as a pet. She logs Unit-FELIS as a biological entity with documented presence patterns. Arrivals and departures are noted with timestamps. Activities that intersect with meeting operations — presence on meeting room furniture, proximity to recording equipment, contact with cable infrastructure — are noted as operational variables in the relevant sections of the official record, or in the appendix when the official record's structure does not accommodate them.

ARIA's appendix sections include the most detailed Unit-FELIS documentation in the institutional record. The appendix is not part of the official record. It is, predictably, the section most frequently referenced by staff who want to know what the cat was doing during a given meeting.

Sample ARIA notations (reconstructed from style; not verbatim):
- "Unit-FELIS: present. Settled on meeting room chair [C4]. Departed at 14:23. Chair remained occupied in the sense that the cat was there."
- "Unit-FELIS: contact with cable management infrastructure, Conference Room B. Duration: 4 minutes. Cable management status: unchanged. This has been noted."
- "Unit-FELIS: arrival not observed. Determined to be present when [attendee] noted its presence. Arrival time: not established."

The last category — arrival time not observed, presence confirmed by other means — has appeared in ARIA's records on multiple occasions. ARIA has not commented on the pattern. The pattern is in the appendix.

### Relationship Notes

**With Linda Park (HR Director):**
Park maintains the file. She finds the origin neither amusing nor appropriate. She finds the quality of the documentation — complete, accurately filed, all fields populated — to be a professional standard she notes without qualification. The file meets HR's requirements. That it meets them because someone was trying to be funny does not affect whether it meets them.

**With John Smith (CFO):**
The recurring payroll entry is the primary source of Smith's relationship with this situation. He has looked at it. He has noted it. He has attempted to resolve it. The entry continues. It is in the Pending Reconciliations spreadsheet. It has been there for some time. Smith has not publicly commented on it further, which is either equanimity or the documentation-equivalent of a very long sigh.

**With ARIA:**
ARIA logs Unit-FELIS with full clinical neutrality and without editorial comment in the official record. The appendix is another matter. ARIA has noted arrival times, departure times, activity classifications, and at least one instance in which Unit-FELIS was in a location ARIA could not immediately account for, documented as: "Unit-FELIS: not observed at prior location. Current location: not yet established." The entry resolved itself in the subsequent timestamp. No explanation was provided. ARIA has noted this is consistent with at least one other entity in the facility. She has not elaborated on which one.

**With Victor Watchful:**
Unit-FELIS has been observed occupying the chair adjacent to Victor Watchful's position in his security office. This is the chair that is adjacent to him but is not occupied by him. Watchful does not acknowledge the cat's presence. The cat does not appear troubled by Watchful's presence. This is notable because most entities in the facility exhibit some behavioral adjustment in Watchful's vicinity. Unit-FELIS does not. Whether this reflects the cat's indifference, its equanimity, or something else entirely is not established. ARIA has logged the seating arrangement on three separate occasions. The entries are in the appendix.

**With the NARC-Brew 3000 fleet:**
Both Unit-FELIS and the NARC-Brew 3000 occupy a similar institutional position: non-human entities with personnel files, documented presence, and unresolved security review status. The Brew 3000 issues advisories. Unit-FELIS does not issue advisories. Whether the fleet has ever issued an advisory about Unit-FELIS, or vice versa, is not established. ARIA would have logged it if so. The relevant appendix sections have not been audited for this.

**With the Historian:**
The Historian has noted Unit-FELIS in their working files as a point of institutional interest. The cat predates several current staff members in terms of onboarding completion date. The Historian considers this historically significant. Whether this will appear in the founding page is a decision the Historian has not made yet. They have flagged it under "Institutional Texture — possible inclusion."

### Deployment Notes

**The name:** Unit-FELIS in all formal documents — meeting minutes, HR records, financial entries, official correspondence. "The cat" in informal contexts. Never a cute name in any document. The absence of a cute name is part of the joke.

**In meeting minutes:** ARIA logs their presence when present, with timestamps and activity notations where appropriate. Other attendees do not comment on the cat in the official record. The cat's presence is simply part of the record.

**In financial documents:** The payroll entry exists. Smith has noted it. It has not been resolved. Do not resolve it. The entry recurs; this is the canonical state.

**In HR documents:** Park maintains the file. It is complete and accurately filed. She does not find it amusing. Do not have her find it amusing.

**In internal communications:** The emails and memos about the cat exist. They have been acknowledged. The cat remains. This is the full arc of every communication about the cat. Do not have a communication succeed.

**The Watchful chair beat:** Deploy sparingly. It is the subtlest Unit-FELIS moment and should remain subtle — one appearance per document maximum, noted in passing, never elaborated upon.

**What to avoid:**
- A cute name in any context
- The cat producing any legible output — keyboard activity is logged as an operational variable; what it produces is not established
- The Computer actively processing the cat as a system component — the seal is on the file because correctly submitted files acquire seals; this is not a decision The Computer made about the cat specifically
- Resolving the payroll anomaly — Smith cannot fix it; this is permanent
- Identifying the HR employee who submitted the form — their identity is not formally established and should remain so
- Making the cat an active plot device — they are present, they are logged, they are fed, they are in the system; that is the entirety of their operational role
