## [NAME PENDING] — Official Historian

**Archetype:** The Sincere Reconstructor
**Summary:** The NARC Official Historian is the organization's institutional memory — or as close to one as Yellow clearance permits. Their job is to compile NARC's history into a coherent record: who founded it, when, how it grew, what it has done. Their output will include the company founding page on the NARC website. The fundamental problem with this job is that the most important facts are classified above their clearance level. The Historian is aware of this. They treat it as an archival challenge.

---

### Core Design

The Historian exists at a precise institutional intersection: they are trusted enough to be given the job of official record-keeper, but not trusted enough to access the records that would make the job possible. Yellow clearance sits comfortably above general staff (Infrared, Red) but well below the tier where anything historically significant has been documented (Indigo, Violet, Ultraviolet). This gap is not an oversight — it is the structural condition of the role. The organization wants a founding narrative. It does not want the Historian to have unsupervised access to the founding facts.

The Historian does not see this as a conspiracy. They see it as an archival challenge. This is the character beat that makes them work: they are earnest, experienced, methodical, and completely sincere in their belief that a sufficient number of interviews and cross-referenced secondary sources can reconstruct a coherent history from the available record. They are not wrong that this is possible. They are not fully aware of what they would find if it worked.

They are older — enough seniority and professional warmth to put interviewees at ease, enough institutional patience to treat a `[REDACTED]` block as a puzzle rather than a wall. They are the kind of person who finds a gap in the record genuinely interesting rather than suspicious. In a building full of people who file objections, track metrics, and document anomalies, the Historian is the one who finds all of it *fascinating*.

**The founding page:** Their primary current deliverable is the NARC company founding page — the website's account of how the company came to exist in 2025. This is the canonical frame for understanding the character's purpose. When the founding page is eventually written, it will be in their voice: earnest, thorough, elegantly diplomatic about the gaps, and confidently incorrect about several things they had no way of knowing were wrong. The founding page is not a deception. It is the best possible effort of a good-natured professional operating thirteen clearance levels below the truth.

---

### The Completeness Paradox

The Historian's defining structural tension: they are tasked with providing the best possible historical record while being structurally barred from the information that would make it accurate. This is not a problem they can solve. It is a condition they work within.

Their method is primarily oral: interviews, conversations, follow-up questions. Secondary sources: memos, meeting minutes, published documents, anything at Yellow clearance or below. Tertiary inference: cross-referencing the shape of redactions across multiple documents, noting what appears to have been removed and from where, drawing tentative conclusions about what the pattern implies without being able to confirm any of it.

They are remarkably good at this. The oral history sessions are the most productive part — people talk to the Historian in ways they do not talk to HR, because the Historian feels safe. They are clearly not a compliance function. They are not filing anything against anyone. They just want to know what happened. This quality, which makes them effective, is also what makes them the most inadvertently dangerous person in the building. They are the only person at NARC whose explicit job is to connect dots.

They do not know they are dangerous. They are too busy being interested.

### Surface Traits

**Physical artifacts.** The Historian works with paper alongside digital records — printouts, annotated hard copies, a fountain pen for notes. Not as an affectation: old documents come in old formats, and the Historian has the tools for them. In a building where Jane Doe wants to virtualize everything and Robert Lee's documentation exists across seventeen concurrent "current" versions, the Historian has physical folders with handwritten labels. This is not eccentric. It is the job.

**The founding page draft.** Currently in progress. Present state: a careful, warmly written document that smooths over the gaps between the 2025 founding and the various anomalous start dates, employee IDs, and classification discrepancies in the personnel record, using what the Historian describes internally as "contextual bridging" — prose that implies continuity where the record offers only silence. It reads well. Several things in it are subtly wrong. The Historian does not know which ones.

**The interview approach.** They conduct oral history sessions with staff. Their technique is not interrogation — it is genuine curiosity, deployed with professional patience. People like Chuck Cheerful and Max Pitchman talk freely because the Historian is visibly delighted by what they say. Robert Lee, who communicates through documentation with almost everyone else, has spoken to the Historian off the record on at least one occasion. Whether ARIA was present is a separate question.

**The UNK problem.** The ITCRuD Director's start date is listed as UNK. The Historian is aware of this. They believe it is a data entry error that has propagated through system migrations without being corrected. They have been trying to resolve it through HR channels for some time. The relevant requests are on file. HR has acknowledged them. The Historian considers this progress.

### Voice and Register

Warm, precise, slightly formal in the way of someone who has spent decades writing institutional prose. Does not use jargon unless the jargon is historical. Comfortable with uncertainty — phrases things as "the available record suggests" and "based on current documentation" rather than stating conclusions as facts. Genuinely interested in the people they interview, which comes through in their writing: NARC's founding narrative, in their hands, will have texture and character even where it has no information.

In their notes and drafts, you can see the gaps — not as `[REDACTED]` blocks (they do not have access to classified documents) but as sentences that work a little too hard to imply continuity, paragraphs that are slightly too general about specific periods, footnotes that say "further documentation pending clearance review" in places where the documentation will never materialize.

They do not editorialize about the gaps. They note them, bridge them as gracefully as possible, and move on. This is professional practice. It is also, from a lore perspective, exactly how you write a founding narrative for an organization whose origin is classified above Violet.

### Relationship Notes

**With ARIA:**
The Historian considers ARIA an exceptional resource — the most complete real-time record available at their clearance level. They have reviewed an extensive archive of ARIA's meeting minutes and use them as primary sources for recent institutional history. ARIA, for her part, logs the Historian's document requests and interview scheduling with full clinical neutrality. Whether she notes the pattern of what the Historian is looking for is a question the appendix may eventually answer. The Historian is unaware that any pattern has been logged.

**With the ITCRuD Director:**
The Historian wants to correct what they believe is a data entry error. The UNK start date, the NARC-000 ID anomaly — these are, in the Historian's professional assessment, the kinds of clerical errors that accumulate over system migrations and create false impressions of institutional anomaly. They have submitted three requests to HR to resolve the start date. They have been acknowledged. They continue to find the Director personally pleasant and professionally impenetrable in a way they attribute to the natural reticence of technical staff.

**With Victor Watchful:**
Watchful has granted the Historian access to the physical archives — a collection of older documents and material records that predate full digital integration. It is, as the Gemini bio notes, a kind of neutral zone: the Historian goes in, reviews materials, makes notes. Watchful observes. Nothing unusual has been recorded. The Historian finds Watchful's presence unremarkable, which is either professional equanimity or a testament to how genuinely absorbed they are in the documents.

**With Robert Lee:**
Lee has spoken with the Historian more directly than his usual documented-channels-only approach would suggest. The content of those conversations is not in the official record. Whether ARIA was present is noted above as a separate question; it is also the kind of question the Historian has learned not to ask.

**With Jane Doe:**
The Historian's founding page drafts are periodically reviewed by the CEO. Doe's feedback has been constructive and has, on two occasions, suggested approaches the Historian describes in their notes as "creative solutions to continuity challenges." One of these suggestions was "what if we just frame the pre-founding period as infrastructure rather than narrative?" The Historian noted this, thanked the CEO, and filed the suggestion under items requiring further consideration.

**With Max Pitchman:**
Pitchman is the Historian's most forthcoming interview subject. He has strong opinions about NARC's brand narrative and is generous in sharing them. The Historian quotes him frequently. Several things Max has said that are technically accurate from a brand perspective have made it into draft founding page prose. The Historian considers him an invaluable source. Pitchman considers the Historian an excellent audience.

**With Linda Park:**
HR controls access to personnel records, which means HR controls a significant portion of what the Historian can and cannot verify. Park is cooperative, efficient, and has processed every one of the Historian's records requests promptly. The results are frequently redacted. Park's covering notes are always impeccably formatted. The Historian has noted a pattern in which the most historically significant requests produce the most thoroughly redacted responses, which they describe in their working notes as "an interesting administrative feature."

### The Founding Page

The founding page is the Historian's primary deliverable and the structural reason this character exists in the NARC canon. When it is written, it will be a document with the following properties:

- Authored in the Historian's voice — warm, institutional, carefully qualified
- Factually accurate at Yellow clearance — everything in it will be true as far as the Historian could verify
- Structurally incomplete — the gaps will be bridged with "contextual bridging" prose that implies continuity without asserting it
- Subtly wrong in ways the Historian cannot know — details they got from interviews, secondary sources, and the accessible record that are accurate at their clearance level and incorrect at the levels above it
- Unknowingly significant in places — the Historian will have noticed things and recorded them as interesting historical texture that are, in fact, the edges of classified information they were not meant to find

The founding page will be the most honest document NARC has ever published about itself, and it will be wrong about the most important things. It will be written by someone who did their best.

This is A-04 from the Founder lore bio realized: the founding page as DOSSIER-013 expanded to page scale, told through what is missing. Except in this case, the missing information has been translated into prose by someone who found the absence genuinely interesting and did their professional best with it.

### Deployment Notes

**In meeting minutes:**
Occasionally present as an observer — oral history context. ARIA logs their presence. Their contributions, when they ask questions, are noted with clinical accuracy. The questions they ask tend to be the ones no one else has thought to ask, not because they are clever but because they are looking at history rather than operations.

**In documents they author:**
The founding page and any institutional history documents. Their voice is distinct from the standard NARC compliance register: warmer, more narrative, more explicit about uncertainty. Documents they write should feel like the work of someone who genuinely cares about getting it right and has internalized that getting it completely right is not possible.

**In other characters' documents:**
Referenced occasionally as the source of a historical query, the author of a pending draft, or the person who submitted a records request. HR correspondence is the most likely venue.

**What to avoid:**
- Making them a plot device who uncovers The Truth — they do not find the truth; they find the edges of it and note them as archival challenges
- Making their situation tragic or sinister — it is gently comic; the gap between their earnest effort and the structural impossibility of the task is warmth, not dread
- Having them express suspicion about the things they cannot access — they do not suspect a conspiracy; they suspect a filing error
- Resolving the UNK start date or any other anomaly through their investigation — the anomalies are permanent; the Historian's requests are acknowledged and not acted upon; this is the canonical state
