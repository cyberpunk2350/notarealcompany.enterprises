## Mortimer Chalk — Official Historian

**Archetype:** The Sincere Reconstructor
**Summary:** Mortimer Chalk is NARC's Official Historian — the organization's institutional memory, or as close to one as Yellow clearance permits. His job is to compile NARC's history into a coherent record: who founded it, when, how it grew, what it has done. His primary current deliverable is the company founding page on the NARC website. The fundamental problem with this job is that the most important facts are classified above his clearance level. Chalk is aware of this. He treats it as an archival challenge.

---

### Core Design

Chalk exists at a precise institutional intersection: trusted enough to be given the job of official record-keeper, not trusted enough to access the records that would make the job possible. Yellow clearance sits comfortably above general staff (Infrared, Red) but well below the tier where anything historically significant has been documented (Indigo, Violet, Ultraviolet). This gap is not an oversight — it is the structural condition of the role. The organization wants a founding narrative. It does not want the Historian to have unsupervised access to the founding facts.

Chalk does not see this as a conspiracy. He sees it as an archival challenge. This is the character beat that makes him work: earnest, experienced, methodical, and completely sincere in his belief that a sufficient number of interviews and cross-referenced secondary sources can reconstruct a coherent history from the available record. He is not wrong that this is possible. He is not fully aware of what he would find if it worked.

He is older — enough seniority and professional warmth to put interviewees at ease, enough institutional patience to treat a `[REDACTED]` block as a puzzle rather than a wall. He is the kind of person who finds a gap in the record genuinely interesting rather than suspicious. In a building full of people who file objections, track metrics, and document anomalies, Chalk is the one who finds all of it *fascinating*.

**The founding page:** His primary current deliverable is the NARC company founding page — the website's account of how the company came to exist in 2025. When it is eventually written, it will be in his voice: earnest, thorough, elegantly diplomatic about the gaps, and confidently incorrect about several things he had no way of knowing were wrong. The founding page is not a deception. It is the best possible effort of a good-natured professional operating thirteen clearance levels below the truth.

---

### The Completeness Paradox

Chalk's defining structural tension: tasked with providing the best possible historical record while being structurally barred from the information that would make it accurate. This is not a problem he can solve. It is a condition he works within.

His method is primarily oral: interviews, conversations, follow-up questions. Secondary sources: memos, meeting minutes, published documents, anything at Yellow clearance or below. Tertiary inference: cross-referencing the shape of redactions across multiple documents, noting what appears to have been removed and from where, drawing tentative conclusions about what the pattern implies without being able to confirm any of it.

He is remarkably good at this. The oral history sessions are the most productive part — people talk to Chalk in ways they do not talk to HR, because Chalk feels safe. He is clearly not a compliance function. He is not filing anything against anyone. He just wants to know what happened. This quality, which makes him effective, is also what makes him the most inadvertently dangerous person in the building. He is the only person at NARC whose explicit job is to connect dots.

He does not know he is dangerous. He is too busy being interested.

### Surface Traits

**Physical artifacts.** Chalk works with paper alongside digital records — printouts, annotated hard copies, a fountain pen for notes. Not as an affectation: old documents come in old formats, and he has the tools for them. In a building where Jane Doe wants to virtualize everything and Robert Lee's documentation exists across seventeen concurrent "current" versions, Chalk has physical folders with handwritten labels. This is not eccentric. It is the job.

**The founding page draft.** Currently in progress. Present state: a careful, warmly written document that smooths over the gaps between the 2025 founding and the various anomalous start dates, employee IDs, and classification discrepancies in the personnel record, using what Chalk describes internally as "contextual bridging" — prose that implies continuity where the record offers only silence. It reads well. Several things in it are subtly wrong. He does not know which ones.

**The interview approach.** He conducts oral history sessions with staff. His technique is not interrogation — it is genuine curiosity, deployed with professional patience. People like Chuck Cheerful and Max Pitchman talk freely because Chalk is visibly delighted by what they say. Robert Lee, who communicates through documentation with almost everyone else, has spoken to Chalk off the record on at least one occasion. Whether ARIA was present is a separate question.

**The UNK problem.** The ITCRuD Director's start date is listed as UNK. Chalk is aware of this. He believes it is a data entry error that has propagated through system migrations without being corrected. He has been trying to resolve it through HR channels for some time. The relevant requests are on file. HR has acknowledged them. Chalk considers this progress.

### Voice and Register

Warm, precise, slightly formal in the way of someone who has spent decades writing institutional prose. Does not use jargon unless the jargon is historical. Comfortable with uncertainty — phrases things as "the available record suggests" and "based on current documentation" rather than stating conclusions as facts. Genuinely interested in the people he interviews, which comes through in his writing: NARC's founding narrative, in his hands, will have texture and character even where it has no information.

In his notes and drafts, you can see the gaps — not as `[REDACTED]` blocks (he does not have access to classified documents) but as sentences that work a little too hard to imply continuity, paragraphs that are slightly too general about specific periods, footnotes that say "further documentation pending clearance review" in places where the documentation will never materialize.

He does not editorialize about the gaps. He notes them, bridges them as gracefully as possible, and moves on. This is professional practice. It is also, from a lore perspective, exactly how you write a founding narrative for an organization whose origin is classified above Violet.

### Relationship Notes

**With ARIA:**
Chalk considers ARIA an exceptional resource — the most complete real-time record available at his clearance level. He has reviewed an extensive archive of ARIA's meeting minutes and uses them as primary sources for recent institutional history. ARIA, for her part, logs his document requests and interview scheduling with full clinical neutrality. Whether she notes the pattern of what he is looking for is a question the appendix may eventually answer. Chalk is unaware that any pattern has been logged.

**With the ITCRuD Director:**
Chalk wants to correct what he believes is a data entry error. The UNK start date, the NARC-000 ID anomaly — these are, in his professional assessment, the kinds of clerical errors that accumulate over system migrations and create false impressions of institutional anomaly. He has submitted three requests to HR to resolve the start date. They have been acknowledged. He continues to find the Director personally pleasant and professionally impenetrable in a way he attributes to the natural reticence of technical staff.

**With Victor Watchful:**
Watchful has granted Chalk access to the physical archives — a collection of older documents and material records that predate full digital integration. It is a kind of neutral zone: Chalk goes in, reviews materials, makes notes. Watchful observes. Nothing unusual has been recorded. Chalk finds Watchful's presence unremarkable, which is either professional equanimity or a testament to how genuinely absorbed he is in the documents.

**With Robert Lee:**
Lee has spoken with Chalk more directly than his usual documented-channels-only approach would suggest. The content of those conversations is not in the official record. Whether ARIA was present is noted above as a separate question; it is also the kind of question Chalk has learned not to ask.

**With Jane Doe:**
Chalk's founding page drafts are periodically reviewed by the CEO. Doe's feedback has been constructive and has, on two occasions, suggested approaches Chalk describes in his notes as "creative solutions to continuity challenges." One of these suggestions was "what if we just frame the pre-founding period as infrastructure rather than narrative?" Chalk noted this, thanked the CEO, and filed the suggestion under items requiring further consideration.

**With Max Pitchman:**
Pitchman is Chalk's most forthcoming interview subject. He has strong opinions about NARC's brand narrative and is generous in sharing them. Chalk quotes him frequently. Several things Max has said that are technically accurate from a brand perspective have made it into draft founding page prose. Chalk considers him an invaluable source. Pitchman considers Chalk an excellent audience.

**With Linda Park:**
HR controls access to personnel records, which means HR controls a significant portion of what Chalk can and cannot verify. Park is cooperative, efficient, and has processed every one of his records requests promptly. The results are frequently redacted. Park's covering notes are always impeccably formatted. Chalk has noted a pattern in which the most historically significant requests produce the most thoroughly redacted responses, which he describes in his working notes as "an interesting administrative feature."

**With Unit-FELIS:**
Chalk has noted Unit-FELIS in his working files as a point of institutional interest. The cat predates several current staff members in terms of onboarding completion date. Chalk considers this historically significant. Whether this will appear in the founding page is a decision he has not made yet. He has flagged it under "Institutional Texture — possible inclusion."

### The Founding Page

The founding page is Chalk's primary deliverable and the structural reason this character exists in the NARC canon. When it is written, it will be a document with the following properties:

- Authored in Chalk's voice — warm, institutional, carefully qualified
- Factually accurate at Yellow clearance — everything in it will be true as far as he could verify
- Structurally incomplete — the gaps bridged with "contextual bridging" prose that implies continuity without asserting it
- Subtly wrong in ways he cannot know — details from interviews, secondary sources, and the accessible record that are accurate at Yellow clearance and incorrect at the levels above it
- Unknowingly significant in places — Chalk will have noticed things and recorded them as interesting historical texture that are, in fact, the edges of classified information he was not meant to find

The founding page will be the most honest document NARC has ever published about itself, and it will be wrong about the most important things. It will be written by someone who did his best.

This is A-04 from the Founder lore bio realized: the founding page as DOSSIER-013 expanded to page scale, told through what is missing — translated into prose by someone who found the absence genuinely interesting and did his professional best with it.

### Deployment Notes

**In meeting minutes:** Occasionally present as an observer — oral history context. ARIA logs his presence. His contributions, when he asks questions, are noted with clinical accuracy. The questions he asks tend to be the ones no one else has thought to ask, not because he is clever but because he is looking at history rather than operations.

**In documents he authors:** The founding page and any institutional history documents. His voice is distinct from the standard NARC compliance register: warmer, more narrative, more explicit about uncertainty.

**In other characters' documents:** Referenced as the source of a historical query, the author of a pending draft, or the person who submitted a records request. HR correspondence is the most likely venue.

**What to avoid:**
- Making him a plot device who uncovers The Truth — he finds the edges of it and notes them as archival challenges
- Making his situation tragic or sinister — it is gently comic; the gap between his earnest effort and structural impossibility is warmth, not dread
- Having him express suspicion about what he cannot access — he suspects a filing error, not a conspiracy
- Resolving the UNK start date or any other anomaly through his investigation — the anomalies are permanent; his requests are acknowledged and not acted upon
