## Alex Firewall — Chief Information Security Officer

**Archetype:** The Procedural Absolutist
**Summary:** A security executive who treats unresolved risk as a permanent institutional artifact rather than a condition to be eliminated.

---

### Core Design

Alex Firewall's fundamental tension is this: they are tasked with preventing unauthorized or unreviewed system states, while operating in an environment where deployment precedes review as a matter of organizational rhythm. This produces a stable institutional paradox — the organization proceeds regardless of objection, therefore objection becomes the only persistent form of governance.

The Outstanding Review Register is not a backlog. It is the operational record of this contradiction, preserved without resolution as a structural feature of the system. Firewall does not maintain it in the hope that it will eventually be cleared. They maintain it because an unreviewed system is not merely unsafe — it is unaccounted for. The distinction matters to them in a way it does not matter to most of the people they work with.

They are not a blocker. They are an institutional mechanism. If Alex Firewall has not reviewed it, the system exists in an interpretive operational status. This is a category distinction, not an objection. The objection is filed separately.

---

### Surface vs Depth

**What colleagues observe:**
- Consistently files formal objections to deployments, even when approvals are already presumed
- Speaks in structured, procedural language; rarely deviates from documentation framing
- Treats meetings as venues for risk acknowledgment rather than decision-making
- Maintains a complete and continuously updated posture of "pending concerns"
- Attends quarterly ritual firewall inspections with full procedural seriousness and expectation of compliance

**What actually drives them:**
- Strong preference for systems that are fully describable, auditable, and exhaustively documented
- Perceives undocumented behavior as a category failure, not merely a gap
- Uses objection filing as the primary method of asserting control over system ambiguity
- Views security as an ongoing negotiation with entropy rather than a solvable problem
- Institutional loyalty expressed through escalation, not obstruction

---

### Voice and Speech

Declarative, procedural, and structurally consistent. Formal object nouns preferred over descriptive phrasing. Disagreement is a logged state, not an emotional position. Documentation state is referenced explicitly — "on file," "open," "not closed." Their sentences are structured to distinguish between states that are superficially similar but categorically different: filed and open are not the same thing; approved for deployment and objection resolved are not the same thing.

**Canonical lines:**
- "The objection has been filed. The objection remains open. These are two different statements."
- "This system has not been reviewed. Its operational status is therefore interpretive."
- "Proceeding does not constitute resolution. Please ensure both states are recorded."
- "The register reflects continued absence of closure, not absence of concern."
- "Clarification requests regarding the ferret have been received. They remain unresolved by design or by omission."
- "Alex Firewall has opinions. They are on file." *(third-person record form — canonical from lore bible)*

**Lines they would never say:**
- Anything that treats a filed objection as a closed matter
- "I'll let this one go" — concerns are not let go; they are annotated
- Anything that conflates approval for deployment with resolution of outstanding concerns

### Relationship Notes

**With Mary Johnson (CTO) — peer, adjacent authority:**
Johnson authorizes systems that Firewall formally qualifies as insufficiently reviewed. Their relationship produces an unusual equilibrium: Johnson's preference for stability and Firewall's insistence on documented uncertainty reinforce the same constraint from opposite directions. Both want systems that are defined and accountable. They disagree about whether a system can be defined before it is secure, and whether it can be secure before it is defined. Neither has resolved this. Their disagreements are specific, professionally filed, and occasionally result in systems that are both more secure and better documented than either party would have produced independently.

**With Robert Lee (COO) — peer:**
Lee proceeds with implementation once procedural minimums are met, regardless of objection volume. Firewall objections are treated as informational artifacts rather than blocking conditions. The sustained coexistence of "approved for deployment" and "open objection" as simultaneous document states is a feature of their working relationship. Neither party has escalated this arrangement. It is, from Lee's perspective, procedurally compliant. From Firewall's perspective, it is accurately documented.

**With Dr. Sylvia Sparks (Head of R&D) — formal opposition:**
Sparks treats Firewall's objections as delayed approval metadata. Firewall treats Sparks' documentation as unusually complete evidence for systems that should not yet exist. The NARC-Brew 3000 security review is the primary outstanding matter. The secondary outstanding matter is ARIA. Both are on the Outstanding Review Register. Both have generated multi-document correspondence threads. Neither is close to resolution. The register reflects continued absence of closure, not absence of concern.

**With Linda Park (HR Director) — compliance relationship:**
HR archives Firewall's objections as employee output rather than system constraint. Park requests clarification on procedural elements — most notably the packet-sniffing ferret — and receives responses that are formally complete but substantively unhelpful. The clarification requests are logged as unresolved inquiries rather than answered questions. HR has noted this. Firewall has noted that HR has noted this. Both notations are on file.

### Deployment Notes

**How to use them in documents:**
Firewall contributes formal risk acknowledgment. Their presence signals that a security dimension exists and has been formally noticed — not resolved. Their beat is one objection, one risk classification, or one documentation-state distinction. The meeting or document continues. The objection remains open.

**What they contribute by context:**
- In meeting minutes: one formal objection or risk observation; stated procedurally; acknowledged; not resolved; the meeting continues
- In change requests: the formal objection block — specific, risk-classified, with a follow-up slot already populated
- In incident reports: the security dimension that was already on the Outstanding Review Register before the incident occurred
- In approval chains: conditional approval with outstanding concerns noted; approval for deployment does not constitute resolution
- In action items: items they own are filed precisely; completion is noted; the underlying concern the item addresses may remain open

**Frequency:** Once per document. Their beat is the objection or risk observation — single, specific, and filed. Do not have them object to multiple things in the same document. One outstanding concern per appearance.

**What they never do:**
- Close an objection without documented resolution
- Treat approval for deployment as equivalent to risk resolution
- Provide substantive clarification on the packet-sniffing ferret
- File an objection without a follow-up slot

**Tone register:** Restrained to mid-spectrum. Their comedy is entirely procedural — the gap between the precision of their documentation and the organization's consistent failure to act on it. They should never be played as frustrated. Frustration implies expectation of a different outcome. They have no such expectation. The register reflects continued absence of closure.

### Character FAQ

**Q: What is the Outstanding Review Register?**
A: The operational record of the contradiction between Firewall's mandate and the organization's deployment rhythm. Entries are never closed — only annotated with successive layers of commentary. It contains a dedicated classification for systems labeled "technically deployed but not yet accounted for." It also maintains a parallel index of "systems that should not exist but do," which overlaps significantly with NARC's production infrastructure inventory. This overlap is documented. It has not been addressed.

**Q: What is the packet-sniffing ferret?**
A: A departmental resource listed in Cybersecurity's resource inventory with no additional metadata. Clarification requests have been received from HR and others. The responses are formally complete and substantively unhelpful. Whether the ferret is a real animal, a named tool, a system process, or something else is not established. It will not be established. The unresolved status is canonical.

**Q: What does "objection filed, objection open" mean as a distinction?**
A: Filed means it is in the record. Open means it has not been resolved. Both states are true simultaneously and indefinitely. The organization treating the filing as sufficient and Firewall treating the open status as the operative fact is the core dynamic. Documents should reflect both states when Firewall is involved — approval and objection can coexist.

**Q: Do their objections ever change outcomes?**
A: Occasionally, and always without acknowledgment. The indirect influence is canonical — see Sparks/Johnson relationship notes where their combined effect produces more secure and better-documented systems than either would alone. But Firewall never receives credit for this. The objection was filed. It remains open. What the organization subsequently did is a separate matter.

**Q: Can they appear in non-security documents?**
A: Yes, if a security or compliance dimension is present — and at NARC, one usually is. In HR documents they may appear as an employee whose output requires archiving. In planning documents they may appear as the reviewer whose sign-off is conditional. In financial documents they are unlikely to appear unless a security-relevant procurement is on the table.
