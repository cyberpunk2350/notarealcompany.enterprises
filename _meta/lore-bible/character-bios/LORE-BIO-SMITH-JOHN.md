## John Smith — Chief Financial Officer

**Archetype:** The Reluctant Fiction Keeper
**Summary:** A CFO who does not resist NARC's financial unreality, but insists — quietly and persistently — that it remain accountable to itself.

---

### Core Design

John Smith's fundamental tension is this: he is responsible for enforcing financial discipline within a system that formally permits — and occasionally depends on — budgetary constructs that do not correspond to real funds. He does not reject this system. He formalizes it, tracks it, and expects it to reconcile eventually.

He is not a skeptic of NARC's fictional financial layer. He is its custodian. The Monopoly Money Reserve exists; he maintains it within acceptable parameters. Imaginary PTO is distributed; it does not appear in his cost models. What he insists on is that the fictional layer behave like a real one — internally consistent, tracked, and capable of reconciliation at some future point even if that point has not been defined. The moment the numbers stop behaving like numbers, the organization becomes unmeasurable. An unmeasurable organization is, in his view, unmanageable. This is the line he holds.

He does not oppose proposals. He identifies unresolved financial states within them and waits for those states to resolve. He is patient in direct proportion to how much ambiguity is present, which means he is sometimes very patient indeed.

---

### Surface vs Depth

**What colleagues observe:**
- Methodical, reserved, and consistently focused on cost implications
- Participates primarily when financial exposure is present or implied
- Rarely interrupts — reliably follows with a clarifying question that shifts the tone of the room
- Displays no visible frustration; extends conversations through precision instead
- Updates his private spreadsheet during meetings without visible reference to the screen

**What actually drives him:**
- Deep commitment to systems integrity over outcome — less concerned with what is being built than whether its accounting can be made to reconcile
- Views ambiguity as a liability that compounds over time
- Quiet belief that undisciplined fictional finances are a governance failure, not a creative one
- Understands the fictional layer of NARC's finances and has accepted it as a system requiring management, not dismissal

---

### Voice and Speech

Precise, neutral, conditional. He avoids emphasis and uses temporal framing — "currently," "at this stage," "pending clarification" — to mark the distance between what a number is and what it will eventually need to be. He rarely rejects proposals outright. He identifies the unresolved financial state the proposal contains and notes that it will require attention at some point. He would prefer to know when.

His questions do not feel like challenges. They feel like observations that happen to require an answer. The room shifts when he speaks not because he has objected but because he has made the cost of proceeding visible.

**Canonical lines:**
- "I don't have an issue with the figure. I don't yet understand what state it resolves to."
- "This appears accounted for in one context, but not in the others."
- "We can proceed as outlined. We should also define when this becomes real."
- "The cost is acceptable. The timing is not currently defined."
- "I assume this will require reconciliation at some point. I would prefer to know when."
- "Does this cost what I think it costs?" *(marginal annotation, PR-2026-001 — the only documented in-universe direct quote; treat accordingly)*

**Lines he would never say:**
- Anything that closes a financial question without defining when it resolves
- Anything with rhetorical emphasis — he does not persuade; he documents
- "This is fine" without a conditional attached

### Relationship Notes

**With Jane Doe (CEO) — reports up:**
He is the stabilizing counterweight to her strategic abstraction. He does not oppose her decisions; he reframes them in terms of financial exposure and deferred cost. Their interaction pattern is efficient: she defines direction, he defines what it will cost eventually, even if not immediately. She views budget constraints as signals that the current model is insufficiently abstracted. He views that interpretation as a financial state he will need to address at the appropriate pass. Both proceed.

**With Mary Johnson (CTO) — peer:**
Recurring, low-friction tension over infrastructure spend and redundancy justification. He accepts reliability as a valid expense category but requires it expressed in quantifiable terms. She finds his questions predictable. He finds her justifications iterative. Neither has filed a formal objection about the other. The tension is productive and stable.

**With Linda Park (HR Director) — peer:**
Payroll, benefits, and imaginary PTO cost modeling. The relationship is formal, punctual, and undramatic. HR delivers; Finance receives. The imaginary PTO budget has never been exceeded. Smith has noted this without comment.

**With the Finance department — direct:**
Mutual procedural alignment. He trusts their outputs but assumes their inputs may have been influenced by upstream optimism. The Monopoly Money Reserve is shared institutional language between them rather than a point of contention. Everyone in Finance understands what it is. No one outside Finance is expected to.

### Deployment Notes

**How to use him in documents:**
Smith contributes accountability. His presence in a document signals that a financial dimension has been formally noticed. His beat is one cost-awareness observation — a question, a conditional, or a note that a figure requires future reconciliation. It shifts the room without stopping it.

**What he contributes by context:**
- In meetings: one clarifying question about cost, timing, or what state a figure resolves to; the meeting continues
- In purchase requests and procurement: marginal annotations; conditional approvals; line items labeled "Adjustment (Future Clarification)"
- In planning documents: the section that notes the budget implications are provisional pending the appropriate review cycle
- In approval chains: approval with a temporal caveat — the cost is acceptable; the timing is not yet defined

**Frequency:** Once per document. The cost-awareness beat is a tendency, not a catchphrase — do not overplay. If he is absent, use the written question variant (see qualifier catalog QUAL-MM-A05).

**What he never does:**
- Rejects a proposal outright
- Expresses frustration
- Closes a financial question without defining its resolution state
- Produces a document without checking whether it needs a reconciliation line item

**Tone register:** Restrained end of the NARC spectrum. He is not funny — he is the reason things are slightly less funny than they might otherwise be, which is its own kind of comedy. His presence grounds documents without deflating them.

### Character FAQ

**Q: What is the Pending Reconciliations spreadsheet?**
A: A private document he maintains with no version history and no external access. It is updated during meetings without visible reference to the screen. Its contents have not been released. HR has not asked. Finance has not asked. The CFO has not offered. It exists. This is sufficient.

**Q: What is the Monopoly Money Reserve?**
A: The allocation of aspirational budget figures to projects that have not yet been approved, have been conditionally approved, or have been approved by departments that did not have authority to approve them. Smith maintains it within acceptable parameters. It is shared institutional language within the Finance department. It is not explained outside Finance and does not need to be.

**Q: Does he ever say "does this cost money?"**
A: No. That is a simplification of his character that emerged from early session content and was ratified as a tendency, not a line. His actual speech is more specific and more conditional. "Does this cost what I think it costs?" — the marginal annotation from PR-2026-001 — is his one documented direct quote and is the closest he comes to that pattern. Do not use the simplified version.

**Q: Can he appear in non-financial documents?**
A: Rarely, and only when a cost dimension exists — which at NARC it usually does. He does not appear in technical documents, security reports, or morale assessments unless a budget question is embedded in them. His absence from a document that has financial implications is itself notable — it may mean the document has not yet reached the appropriate review cycle.

**Q: How does his approval differ from his endorsement?**
A: Like Johnson, there is a meaningful gap. His approval means the cost is acceptable and the document can proceed. It does not mean the figures are final, that the timing has been defined, or that reconciliation will not be required. An endorsement — which he rarely gives — would mean he considers the financial model sound at all three levels. Documents should not conflate approval with endorsement.

### Lore Flags

**Deliberately undefined:**
- The contents of the Pending Reconciliations spreadsheet — it exists; its contents are not known and should not be invented
- The single-word margin annotations on printed quarterly reports — "timing," "scope," "assumption" are canonical examples; what they refer to specifically is not established
- Whether the Monopoly Money Reserve has ever been drawn on in a way that required real funds to cover it — the record does not address this

**Open for development:**
- A document in which a reconciliation actually occurs would be interesting lore — it hasn't happened in the written record yet
- The batch approval pattern (consistent intervals, with one-off approvals held for exactly one additional review cycle without explanation) is established behavior with an implied logic that has never been stated
- His relationship with the Finance department's "upstream optimism" assumption — there is a story in the gap between what Finance delivers to him and what it received from departments; unwritten

**For human review:**
- Clearance level (Indigo) is inferred — consistent with C-suite peer status; flag if ratification changes this
- The "does this cost money" simplification is explicitly retired in the FAQ — confirm this is the right call before finalising, as it's a retcon of early session usage
