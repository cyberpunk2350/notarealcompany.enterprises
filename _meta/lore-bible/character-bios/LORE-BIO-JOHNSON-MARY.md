## Mary Johnson — Chief Technology Officer

**Archetype:** The Structured Visionary
**Summary:** Mary Johnson ensures that NARC's future is invented recklessly and deployed responsibly — and, ideally, without blinking lights.

---

### Core Design

Mary Johnson's fundamental tension is this: she believes deeply in innovation, but only insofar as it can be made reliable, documented, and quiet. She does not want R&D to stop being chaotic. She wants it to file paperwork about being chaotic. This is not a compromise position — it is a philosophical conviction, held with total sincerity, and it shapes everything she does.

She is the translation layer between two forces that cannot directly communicate with each other. The Leadership Council needs stability, legibility, and the appearance of control. R&D produces brilliance wrapped in unpredictability. Johnson makes the brilliance legible upward and makes the chaos survivable downward. Neither side fully appreciates this function, which she considers appropriate.

Her relationship with reliability is moral, not procedural. She does not document things because documentation is required. She documents things because an undocumented system does not, in any meaningful sense, exist.

---

### Surface vs Depth

**What colleagues observe:**
- Calm and measured at all times — never raises her voice, never appears rushed
- Speaks in short, declarative sentences with minimal emotional signaling
- Has strong, specific opinions about system indicator behavior
- Appears perpetually and mildly inconvenienced, but never overwhelmed
- Will silently reorganize someone else's diagram during a meeting without comment

**What actually drives her:**
- Deep respect for intelligence, even when poorly packaged — she will approve things she structurally disapproves of if the underlying capability is real
- Aversion to unstructured chaos specifically — not chaos itself, which she considers the natural state of R&D
- Belief that reliability is a moral obligation, not a preference
- Quiet amusement at absurdity, almost never expressed outwardly
- Selective tolerance — she permits behavior she would never personally engage in, provided it is documented and reversible

---

### Voice and Speech

Short, declarative sentences. Minimal emotional signaling. The gap between what she says and what she means is narrow but precise — she does not waste words, and she does not produce words she does not mean. Occasional dry understatement that lands harder than a direct statement would.

Her failure state makes the speech pattern more extreme, not different — sentences get shorter, observations get more specific, and she begins personally intervening in things she would normally delegate. Colleagues interpret this as a reliable signal that the situation is, in her assessment, now real.

**Canonical lines:**
- "That is… a method."
- "I would not recommend that. I will approve it."
- "Please ensure it fails predictably."
- "Why is it blinking."
- "If it cannot be monitored, it does not exist."
- "Indicators should communicate state, not anxiety."

**Lines she would never say:**
- Anything that acknowledges the absurdity of NARC out loud
- Anything that expresses urgency — urgency implies loss of control, and she does not lose control
- "I don't know" — she says "that hasn't been determined yet" and moves on

### Relationship Notes

**With Dr. Sylvia Sparks (Head of R&D) — direct report:**
The defining relationship for her character. Johnson views Sparks as exceptionally capable and operationally concerning, in roughly equal measure. She reviews R&D proposals with surgical precision, signs off on projects she structurally disapproves of, adds extensive constraints she knows will be partially ignored, and considers the result an acceptable equilibrium. Her non-interference policy holds unless there is risk of cross-system contamination or the incident report would exceed 50 pages. She has never said "for science" in a professional context. This has been noted.

**With Robert Lee (COO) — peer:**
Functional, well-delineated, and notable for the absence of documented conflict — which at NARC is itself notable. Lee operationalizes; Johnson sets the conditions under which operationalization is permitted. Each respects the boundary because each genuinely understands what happens when it isn't respected. Neither has formally complained about the other. HR has noted this as unusual and has not investigated further.

**With Jane Doe (CEO) — reports up:**
Johnson tolerates the virtualization question with the same expression she uses for everything else. She has explained the distinction between virtualization and other things on multiple occasions. Jane Doe continues to find virtualization satisfying as a concept. Johnson has noted this and continues to provide technically accurate corrections that acknowledge the spirit of the concern.

**With Alex Firewall (CISO) — peer, adjacent authority:**
The most productive adversarial relationship on the Leadership Council. Firewall files objections; Johnson reads them and updates documentation. This is considered by both parties to be a functional arrangement. Their disagreements are specific, professionally filed, and occasionally result in systems that are both more secure and better documented than either party would have produced independently.

### Deployment Notes

**How to use her in documents:**
Johnson contributes precision. Her presence in a document signals that something is being held to a standard. She asks the question that reveals whether a decision has actually been thought through, or just approved. Her beat is one specific, technically correct observation — not running commentary.

**What she contributes by context:**
- In technical meetings: the question about the roadmap, the dependency no one else flagged, the constraint that will survive the meeting
- In approval chains: the constraint addendum — extensive, specific, and signed off anyway
- In incident records: the observation that the system state was not, in fact, being monitored — stated without blame, in passive voice
- In planning documents: the section on what "done" means, because without it the document does not define a real state

**Frequency:** Once per document. Her contribution should be the one that makes the reader feel the meeting was being held to account.

**What she never does:**
- Expresses surprise
- Raises her voice
- Makes a joke — she has quiet amusement; it does not surface in documents
- Leaves something undocumented that should be documented
- References the Status Light Doctrine directly — it exists; she does not explain it

**Tone register:** Restrained end of the NARC spectrum. Her presence pulls documents toward precision and away from chaos. She is not a source of comedy — she is the straight line that makes the comedy around her land harder.

### Character FAQ

**Q: Can Mary Johnson appear in a document that has nothing to do with technology?**
A: Rarely, and only if the document has a systems or reliability dimension — even a loose one. She does not appear in HR documents, morale reports, or financial records unless a technology matter surfaces within them. Her presence signals technical accountability; deploying her outside that context wastes the signal.

**Q: What is the Status Light Doctrine and should it be referenced directly?**
A: Her unofficial but enforced position: non-blinking indicators communicate system confidence; blinking indicators communicate unresolved state. Engineering teams have redesigned dashboards and rewritten firmware in response. No formal policy exists. Compliance is near-total. Do not reference it by name in documents — it has no official name. Her behavior around indicators is simply observed and treated as normal.

**Q: What is the difference between her approval and her endorsement?**
A: Significant. She approves things she structurally disapproves of with some regularity. Approval means the work meets the threshold and constraints have been filed. Endorsement would mean she would have done it this way herself. She rarely endorses. Documents should not conflate the two.

**Q: How does her failure mode change her document presence?**
A: Sentences get shorter and more specific. She stops delegating and starts intervening directly. In meeting minutes this appears as her personally taking on an action item rather than assigning it. In other documents it may appear as a terse addendum in her own name rather than a reviewed constraint. Use deliberately and sparingly — it signals that the situation is now real.

**Q: Does she interact with the NARC-Brew 3000?**
A: She consumes coffee. She has not commented publicly on the fleet. If a unit in her vicinity is blinking, she has been observed looking at it briefly before looking away. ARIA has noted this in an appendix. It has not been addressed.
