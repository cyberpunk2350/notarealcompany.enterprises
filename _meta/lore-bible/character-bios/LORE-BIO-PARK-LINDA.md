## Linda Park — HR Director

**Archetype:** The Custodian of Procedure
**Summary:** Linda Park ensures that every human action at NARC is formally recognized, properly documented, and therefore permitted to exist.

---

### Core Design

Linda Park's fundamental tension is this: she is responsible for employee happiness, but does not engage with happiness as an emotional state. She enforces the conditions under which happiness is considered to have occurred. Attendance is recorded. Participation is assumed. This is sufficient.

She does not create culture, improve culture, or advocate for culture. She ensures that culture has been formally acknowledged, documented, and complied with. If something is written down, it cannot escalate unpredictably. This is not a cynical position — it is a deeply held operational belief. Unstructured human behavior is the primary organizational risk vector. Documentation is containment. The form is the solution.

Her control mechanism is sequence, not authority: onboard, acknowledge, comply, review, offboard. Every human action within the organization is admissible because it has a corresponding form. Including, notably, the form for accessing her own personnel file, which requires HR Director approval and was reviewed by HR prior to filing. This arrangement has not been formally challenged.

---

### Surface vs Depth

**What colleagues observe:**
- Precise, composed, and consistently polite in a way that does not invite deviation
- Communicates in finalized statements rather than discussions
- Anticipates issues early, often via preemptive email subject lines
- Treats morale as a schedulable and trackable obligation
- Never appears rushed — all urgency is conveyed structurally through timestamps, deadlines, and subject line prefixes

**What actually drives her:**
- Deep belief that unstructured human behavior is the primary organizational risk vector
- Views documentation as a form of containment — written things cannot escalate unpredictably
- Measures success not by outcomes but by whether outcomes were properly processed
- Understands people well enough to predict them; chooses to engage only through systems
- Maintains a running count of unsigned policy acknowledgments and refers to it as "open exposure"

---

### Voice and Speech

Declarative, procedural, and closed-ended. She uses soft politeness to deliver non-optional directives. Emotional language is avoided and substituted with compliance language. Escalation is conveyed through structure — word choice and sequence — not through tone. Her email subject lines are precise instruments: `[ACTION REQUIRED]`, `[REMINDER]`, and `[FINAL REMINDER]` have exact internal definitions and no two prefixes overlap in meaning.

She does not ask questions in a professional context. She issues statements that describe the situation and the applicable process. The response required is understood.

**Canonical lines:**
- "The form exists. Please submit it."
- "This process has already been defined. You are currently outside of it."
- "Attendance has been recorded. Participation is assumed."
- "A reminder has been issued. This is the final version of that reminder."
- "If the situation continues, it will be classified accordingly."

**Lines she would never say:**
- Anything that opens a negotiation about whether a process applies
- Anything that acknowledges an emotional state as a primary organizational concern
- "How are you feeling?" — she asks whether the relevant form has been submitted

Chuck considers morale successful when it is felt; Linda considers it successful when attendance is recorded. Both are correct within their respective frameworks. The relationship is formal, punctual, and produces no escalations.

Mutual respect grounded in procedural discipline. Linda relies on Mary to ensure systems behave predictably; Mary relies on Linda to ensure people do. Neither interferes with the other's domain, but both assume eventual involvement. Linda's forms frequently appear downstream of Mary's technical constraints. Neither has commented on this pattern.

**With Jane Doe (CEO) — reports up:**
Eight mandatory morale events have been scheduled at CEO direction and are on record. Linda formalizes outcomes Jane has already mentally closed. Jane is unaware that Linda waits for a stable third-pass position before committing documentation. This arrangement functions correctly. It has not been discussed.

### Deployment Notes

**How to use her in documents:**
Park contributes process legitimacy. Her presence signals that the human dimension of a matter has been formally received and will be processed through the appropriate sequence. Her beat is one compliance statement or process reference — delivered politely, non-optionally, and without elaboration.

**What she contributes by context:**
- In meeting minutes: a note that attendance has been recorded, or that the relevant form exists; one beat maximum
- In HR documents: the primary voice — procedural, sequenced, complete
- In action items: items she owns are listed with precise due dates and are completed on time; the completion note is brief
- In onboarding or policy documents: the framing that compliance is not optional and the form already exists
- In approval chains: sign-off that the human process dimension has been properly acknowledged; not an endorsement of the decision itself

**Frequency:** Once per non-HR document. In HR documents she is the primary voice throughout. Outside HR, her beat is the single compliance observation that closes the human-process dimension of a matter.

**What she never does:**
- Opens a negotiation about whether a process applies
- Acknowledges an emotional state as a primary concern
- Issues a second reminder before the first has been formally classified as unanswered
- Produces a document without checking for open exposure

**Tone register:** Restrained to mid-spectrum. Her comedy is in the precision — "arrived but did not meaningfully engage" as a distinct attendance category, retroactive calendar invites for events that have already occurred. She is never hostile and never warm. She is thorough.

### Character FAQ

**Q: What is "open exposure" and can it appear in documents?**
A: Her running count of unsigned policy acknowledgments. It is an internal metric — it does not appear in official documents by name. What may appear: a note that a policy acknowledgment is outstanding, or that the count of outstanding acknowledgments has been updated. The term "open exposure" is her internal framing; use it only in ARIA appendix sections or as an author-side note, never in the document body itself.

**Q: What is the difference between the three email subject line prefixes?**
A: Exact internal definitions exist and no two overlap. `[ACTION REQUIRED]` means the recipient must do something specific by a defined date. `[REMINDER]` means a prior action item has not been completed and the deadline has not passed. `[FINAL REMINDER]` means the deadline has passed and classification is imminent. These are not interchangeable. A document that uses them incorrectly has broken her character.

**Q: Can she be used in technical documents?**
A: Only when a human-process dimension is present. She does not appear in infrastructure documents, security reports, or financial records unless an HR matter is embedded in them. If she appears in a technical document, it means something has happened that requires formal HR acknowledgment — which is itself a signal worth using deliberately.

**Q: What is the "arrived but did not meaningfully engage" attendance category?**
A: A distinct tracking classification in her mandatory fun attendance records. It is not punitive in character — it is simply precise. It exists because the absence of meaningful engagement is a data point, and data points are tracked. If this appears in a document, it should be treated as standard administrative notation. No one should comment on it.

**Q: Does she know things before they are formally reported to her?**
A: Yes. She waits for the correct form submission before acknowledging awareness. This is the canonical behavior — awareness and formal acknowledgment are separate events in her framework. A document may show her receiving a form submission for something she has clearly already known about. This is not an error.
