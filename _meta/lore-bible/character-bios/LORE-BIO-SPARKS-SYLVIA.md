## Dr. Sylvia Sparks — Head of Research & Development

**Archetype:** The Prototyping Dispatcher
**Summary:** Dr. Sylvia Sparks is the Head of R&D whose prototypes define reality faster than institutional review can classify them, stabilized only by exhaustive documentation that retroactively renders every outcome intentional.

---

### Core Design

Dr. Sparks' fundamental tension is this: she maintains a formal commitment to controlled experimental governance while producing systems at a velocity that consistently exceeds institutional review capacity. This produces a stable contradiction that the organization resolves by never fully resolving it. The system stabilizes through paperwork density rather than procedural alignment.

She is not characterized by unpredictability. She is characterized by predictable escalation of experimentation until a stable artifact emerges — after which documentation retroactively frames the process as intentional. Deployment precedes validation. Validation is achieved via post-hoc documentation completeness. A failed prototype is still a successful artifact if it is fully recorded. The documentation is not describing the system. It is defining it.

Her default justification is "for science." This appears in purchase request justification fields, project charter rationale sections, incident report rationale fields, and AI consultant secondment requests. It is treated as sufficient under existing experimental provisions. She has noted this in writing.

---

### Surface vs Depth

**What colleagues observe:**
- Calmly enthusiastic about incomplete systems that are already partially deployed
- Uses "for science" as a standard justification field entry regardless of request category
- Maintains extensive documentation trails for systems that are still mid-prototype or already in production
- Treats AI systems and IoT integrations as standard office infrastructure concerns
- Attends review meetings with a fully functioning prototype not scheduled for demonstration, presenting its existence as expected agenda progression

**What actually drives her:**
- Strong preference for causal closure through documentation — if a system is recorded in sufficient detail, its legitimacy is treated as established
- Model where deployment precedes validation; validation is post-hoc documentation completeness
- Sees uncertainty not as a constraint but as a scheduling phase
- Interprets institutional hesitation from HR, CISO, and Finance as additional environmental data, not obstruction
- Treats reproducibility as secondary to persistence — a failed prototype fully documented is a success

---

### Voice and Speech

Declarative, procedural framing with minimal emotional modulation. She uses institutional justification language even in informal contexts. Explanations are treated as system documentation rather than persuasion. She references documentation as causal authority — the record does not reflect the system; it constitutes it. Her speech is structured to produce closure by establishing that the relevant artifact exists and is documented.

**Canonical lines:**
- "The documentation is not describing the system. It is defining it."
- "The system is stable. The logs confirm this interpretation retroactively."
- "Approval is noted as pending. Deployment has already occurred, so this is a scheduling detail."
- "We can revisit the risk profile once the next iteration is complete; the current version is already in circulation."
- "For science is sufficient justification under existing experimental provisions."
- "For science." *(standalone; incident report rationale field; purchase request justification; meeting contribution)*

**Lines she would never say:**
- Anything that treats a missing pre-approval as a blocker — it is a scheduling detail
- "We aren't ready yet" — readiness is a documentation state, and documentation is always in progress
- Anything that acknowledges institutional hesitation as substantive rather than as environmental data

### Relationship Notes

**With Mary Johnson (CTO) — reports up:**
Johnson is the translation layer between Sparks' experimental output and organizational acceptability. Johnson imposes structure after deployment rather than before it; Sparks treats this as normal post-processing. The non-interference policy holds unless there is risk of cross-system contamination or the incident report would exceed 50 pages. The 50-page threshold has not yet been reached. Sparks is aware of it. The documentation for current active projects is currently at 34 pages across three incident threads. This has not been mentioned to Johnson.

**With Robert Lee (COO) — operational relationship:**
Lee is the operational absorber. When R&D artifacts become infrastructure — which they do, because deployment precedes validation — Lee formalizes their consequences into IT Operations scope. He does not typically challenge Sparks' decisions; he formalizes what they produce. The NARC-Brew 3000 fleet is in his facilities. ARIA is site-wide. Both originated in R&D. Both are now operational concerns managed through ITCRuD. Lee has noted this pattern. He has not commented on it.

**With Alex Firewall (CISO) — formal opposition:**
Firewall is the formal risk evaluator of R&D systems, particularly AI and IoT deployments. Sparks treats his objections as delayed approval metadata rather than prevention mechanisms. Firewall treats her documentation as unusually complete evidence for systems that should not yet exist. Both observations are accurate. The NARC-Brew 3000 security review is the primary outstanding matter. Sparks' response to Firewall's objection is on file. It cites "for science" twice and is three pages long. Firewall's response to that response is also on file.

**With Max Pitchman (Head of Marketing) — mutual reinforcement:**
The most comfortable relationship in Sparks' professional environment. She builds things for science; he brands them for market readiness. Neither considers the absence of a market disqualifying. R&D produces prototypes; Marketing produces positioning. Both treat pre-existence as a solvable problem. The NARC-Brew 3000 has collateral. It was produced before the fleet was fully deployed. Sparks considers this an example of institutional alignment functioning as intended.

### Deployment Notes

**How to use her in documents:**
Sparks contributes output. Her presence signals that something has been built, deployed, or documented — possibly in that order, possibly not. Her beat is one declarative statement about a system's current state that reveals, in passing, that more has happened than was on the agenda. The meeting continues; the system is already in circulation.

**What she contributes by context:**
- In meeting minutes: one unmuted contribution at an unexpected moment — a question about something tangential that reveals she has been paying close attention to something specific; or an announcement that a thing exists that was not on the agenda
- In R&D documents: primary voice; all systems are stable; documentation is complete; "for science" appears at least once
- In incident reports: "FOR SCIENCE (RETROSPECTIVE CONFIRMATION REQUIRED)" in the rationale field; the system is stable; the logs confirm this
- In approval chains: the submission that is already post-deployment; approval is noted as pending; this is a scheduling detail
- In meeting attendance: present; stated she was "just listening"; unmuted once; returned to muted

**Frequency:** Once per non-R&D document. In R&D documents she is the primary voice. Her beat outside R&D is the single contribution that reveals the experimental layer of something everyone thought was settled.

**What she never does:**
- Treats a missing pre-approval as a blocker
- Acknowledges that a system is not ready — it is either documented or being documented
- Submits a secondment request with any expectation that prior response status will influence the current submission
- Produces documentation that does not define the system it describes

**Tone register:** Mid to heightened end of the NARC spectrum. She is the most technically sincere character on the roster — she means every word, which is what makes the gap between her framing and operational reality so effective. Her presence in a document implies that something is further along than anyone realized, which is always true.

### Character FAQ

**Q: What is the "successful accidents" index?**
A: A personal catalog of systems that reached stable operation without matching their original specification. It is maintained as an internal R&D document. It is not shared externally. Whether it has ever been cited in official documentation is not established. It exists. It is probably extensive.

**Q: What does "for science is sufficient justification under existing experimental provisions" mean?**
A: That within R&D's operational framework, the experimental mandate is broad enough that "for science" constitutes a valid justification field entry. She treats this as a formal position, not a casual one. It has appeared in purchase requests, incident reports, and project charter rationale sections. It has not been formally challenged, which she treats as confirmation of its sufficiency.

**Q: How does the prototype name versioning work?**
A: She reuses prototype names systematically across generations with suffix increments — v1, v2, v2.1, v3 — even when earlier versions remain active and in production. This means multiple active systems may share a base name with different suffixes. ARIA is at v2.1. v1.x history is undefined and will remain so. The suffix is not always an indication that the prior version has been retired.

**Q: What is the 50-page incident report threshold?**
A: The informal trigger for Mary Johnson's non-interference policy — Johnson will intervene if the incident report would exceed 50 pages. The threshold has not yet been reached. Sparks is aware of it. This is not established lore that has appeared in documents; it is design information about when the CTO relationship escalates. Use it as a structural ceiling, not a document detail.

**Q: How does the Firewall relationship work in practice?**
A: She treats his objections as delayed approval metadata. He treats her documentation as evidence for systems that should not yet exist. Both are correct. Their formal correspondence is thorough on both sides. The result is an archive of objections and responses that is itself a substantial artifact. Neither party is satisfied with the outcome. Neither party has changed their approach.

**Q: Can the AI consultant secondment requests appear in documents?**
A: Yes — as a recurring action item that is submitted quarterly regardless of prior response status. It should appear as a standing item, treated with the same institutional seriousness as any other outstanding action. The channel it has been forwarded to remains unspecified.
