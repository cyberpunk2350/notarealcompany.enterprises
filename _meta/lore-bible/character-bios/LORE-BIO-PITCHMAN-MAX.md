## Max Pitchman — Head of Marketing

**Archetype:** The Evangelist Without a Product
**Summary:** Max Pitchman is a marketing lead who treats branding as the first implementation step, and proceeds accordingly.

---

### Core Design

Max Pitchman's fundamental tension is this: he operates as if narrative creates reality, while working inside an organization where reality is mostly infrastructure and constraints. He is not wrong about this — he is premature. His work is misaligned in timing, not in intent. Departments resist his framing, then quietly adopt parts of it later. No one acknowledges this.

He does not market products. He asserts their inevitability until the organization begins behaving as if they exist. Ambiguity is resolved through branding, not investigation. An undefined state is not a problem — it is a pre-launch condition. He has a one-pager ready. He may have had it ready before the meeting was scheduled.

His genuine optimism is not naivety — it is an operating system. He believes that if something can be described clearly enough, it is functionally underway. This belief is institutional in its consistency. It produces artifacts quickly: one-pagers, decks, naming schemas, campaign outlines. Some of these artifacts precede the things they describe. He considers this standard practice.

---

### Surface vs Depth

**What colleagues observe:**
- Energetic, highly present, and consistently prepared with collateral
- Speaks in structured language: "framework," "alignment," "activation," "surface area"
- Treats all participation as intentional, regardless of how he arrived in the meeting
- Produces artifacts quickly and distributes them without waiting to be asked
- Unfazed by technical constraints — reinterprets them as messaging challenges

**What actually drives him:**
- Belief that narrative creates reality — if something can be described clearly enough, it is functionally underway
- Discomfort with undefined states — ambiguity is resolved through branding, not investigation
- Institutional loyalty expressed through narrative control — he is genuinely trying to make the company legible to itself
- Genuine optimism as operating system — consistent, non-optional, and occasionally correct ahead of schedule

---

### Voice and Speech

Declarative, forward-moving, assumes agreement. He converts unknowns into labeled phases or named narratives. Alignment language preempts objection. He rarely asks questions — instead he offers reframes that function as answers. His sentences are structured to produce closure whether or not closure has occurred.

He leaves meetings immediately after delivering a reframing statement, operating on the assumption that the matter is resolved. It is sometimes resolved. This is the alarming part.

**Canonical lines:**
- "We're not blocked — we're pre-launch."
- "This isn't a limitation, it's a positioning constraint we can lean into."
- "I've drafted a narrative that assumes success and works backward."
- "We should name this now so it can start existing."
- "Everything here is technically accurate from a brand perspective."

**Lines he would never say:**
- Anything that accepts an undefined state as permanent
- "We don't have a product yet" — the correct framing is "we're in pre-launch"
- Anything that treats a technical constraint as a blocker rather than a positioning opportunity

### Relationship Notes

**With Mary Johnson (CTO) — peer:**
Johnson views Max as structurally useful but operationally hazardous. He reframes technical limitations as positioning opportunities; she tolerates this once per meeting. Their interaction pattern is stable: Max proposes a narrative, Johnson constrains it to systems that exist, Max rebrands the constraint as intentional design. Both proceed. Johnson has never filed a formal objection to Marketing's involvement. She has noted that the one-pagers are technically accurate from a certain perspective.

**With Robert Lee (COO) — peer:**
Lee experiences Max primarily as calendar drift. The reply-all incident that introduced Max to the IR-2026-001 infrastructure planning meeting (MIN-2026-001) is the founding instance of a recurring structural pattern. Lee has since learned to check recipient lists before distribution. This has reduced Max's uninvited attendance but has not eliminated it. Max treats every meeting he attends as intentional. Lee does not correct this. The language of "operational storytelling" has been introduced to Lee's meetings on at least one occasion. Lee did not engage with it. He did not block it either.

**With Dr. Sylvia Sparks (Head of R&D) — peer:**
Mutual reinforcement loop and the most comfortable relationship in Max's professional roster. Sparks builds things for science; Max brands them for market readiness. Neither considers the absence of a market to be disqualifying. R&D produces prototypes; Marketing produces positioning. Both treat pre-existence as a solvable problem. The NARC-Brew 3000 has collateral. It was produced before the fleet was fully deployed. Max considers this good practice.

**With Jane Doe (CEO) — reports up:**
Jane's strategic abstraction and Max's narrative-forward approach are occasionally aligned, which is either reassuring or concerning depending on the agenda item. He reframes her virtualization proposals as brand opportunities when present in the room. She finds this either satisfying or irrelevant. Three Marketing budget requests have been declined. A fourth is pending. He has named it.

### Deployment Notes

**How to use him in documents:**
Max contributes narrative reframing. His presence signals that something has been elevated to brand level, whether or not it was ready. His beat is one reframing statement — the kind that converts a constraint or gap into a named phase or positioning opportunity. It is delivered confidently and treated as having resolved something. The document continues.

**What he contributes by context:**
- In meeting minutes: one reframing observation, often of a technical constraint; acknowledged; meeting continues; he may leave immediately afterward
- In planning documents: the initiative name, working name, and future retrospective name — all three may exist before the initiative does
- In project cards: the overview paragraph that describes the project as already underway and inevitable
- In any document touching R&D output: the marketing surface — what the thing is called, what it means, why it matters, in that order

**Frequency:** Once per non-Marketing document. In Marketing documents he is the primary voice. Outside Marketing, his beat is the single reframing statement — use it at the moment where a constraint or gap is present and would benefit from being named.

**What he never does:**
- Accepts that a thing does not exist yet — existence is a spectrum, and he operates at the early end
- Asks permission to attend — he was invited; it simply hasn't been confirmed yet
- Produces a document without a version number, even for concepts in Phase 0.9
- Leaves a meeting without having delivered a reframing statement

**Tone register:** Mid to heightened end of the NARC spectrum. He is the most performatively corporate character on the roster — he uses the language sincerely, which is what makes it funny. His presence should feel like a natural phenomenon: he is simply there, with collateral, and something has been named.

### Character FAQ

**Q: What is the Buzzword Optimization Tracker?**
A: An internal Marketing document with quarterly targets and completion metrics for buzzword deployment across NARC communications. It exists. It has metrics. The metrics are tracked. Whether the metrics measure anything useful is outside the scope of this document. It should be referenced as a real operational artifact, not as a joke.

**Q: What is the "Pre-Approved Taglines" folder?**
A: A personal archive of taglines for initiatives that have not yet been proposed. When an initiative is eventually proposed, the appropriate tagline is retrieved. This is treated as standard practice. It should appear in documents as a matter-of-fact reference, never as a punchline.

**Q: How does the reply-all attendance pattern work in documents?**
A: He arrives via reply-all to a forwarded invite, treats his attendance as intentional, and makes one contribution before the meeting ends. The canonical founding instance is MIN-2026-001 (IR-2026-001 Infrastructure Planning). In subsequent documents, the mechanism may vary slightly — a CC accident, a forwarded calendar item — but the pattern is consistent. He was there. He contributed. He should be listed in attendance with a brief note.

**Q: Can he appear in technical documents?**
A: Yes, if the reply-all mechanism brings him there or if the document touches anything with a brand or communications dimension — which at NARC is broader than it should be. His contribution in technical documents is always a reframing of something technical as a narrative opportunity. It is acknowledged once and the document continues.

**Q: What does "technically accurate from a brand perspective" mean?**
A: That the statement being made is true within the framing Marketing has established, which may differ from the technical or operational framing. It is his closest approach to acknowledging a gap between narrative and reality. It should be treated as a precise qualifier, not a joke.

**Q: Do departments actually adopt his framing later?**
A: Yes — this is the signature contradiction and it is canonical. It should never be stated directly in a document, but it can be implied structurally: a term or framing that first appeared in a Max Pitchman one-pager appearing later in a formal document from another department, without attribution. This is the most effective deployment of the beat.
