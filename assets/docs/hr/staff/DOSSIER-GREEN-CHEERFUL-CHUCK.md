### ROLE & RESPONSIBILITIES

The Happiness Officer holds accountability for employee morale, mandatory fun programming, surprise snack distribution, and the quantitative measurement of organizational wellbeing via the Happy Packets metric system. Chuck Cheerful operates within the HR department structure and serves as NARC's primary instrument for converting compliance into enthusiasm, or at minimum, converting the absence of enthusiasm into a documented data point.

His formal responsibilities include: morale program design and execution, mandatory fun event scheduling and attendance tracking, snack reserve management (real and imaginary), and the maintenance of the Happy Packets metric — a morale measurement framework he introduced in Year 1, named despite interdepartmental objections, and has since applied to every employee interaction that can be assigned a numerical value. ITCRuD subsequently adopted "Happy Packets" as an internal data integrity metric. Chuck Cheerful considers this a win.

The department motto is "Happiness is Mandatory." This is not a title modifier. It is a cultural statement. The distinction is important to HR. Chuck Cheerful considers the distinction aspirational.

### BEHAVIORAL NOTES

The following observations were compiled from quarterly review materials, morale event attendance logs, Happy Packets metric reports, and ARIA transcripts.

Chuck Cheerful tracks morale with numerical ratings. This is not a metaphor — it is a formal operational practice. Every employee interaction that can be quantified has been quantified. The resulting dataset is maintained by the Happiness Division and has been offered to HR, Finance, and IT Operations for cross-referencing. Only ARIA has accepted the offer. ARIA's acceptance was described by Cheerful as "a Happy Packets score of 9.1."

Mandatory fun events are scheduled, attended, and assessed. Attendance is tracked. Non-attendance is noted. The notes are not punitive in character, which employees are informed of at the start of every mandatory fun event, which Chuck Cheerful considers an important part of the framing.

Surprise snacks are distributed when the budget permits. When the budget does not permit, imaginary snacks are distributed with equivalent ceremony. The morale impact differential between real and imaginary snacks has been studied internally. The results have not been released. Cheerful has stated that releasing them would affect the metric.
