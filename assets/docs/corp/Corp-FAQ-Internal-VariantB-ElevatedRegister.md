## Section 2 — Clearance Levels

**Q: What is my clearance level?**

Your clearance level is on file. Associates who do not know their clearance level should submit a request to HR. HR would like to note that associates who do not know their clearance level represent an interesting data point and will be following up accordingly.

**Q: What do clearance levels mean?**

Clearance levels determine what you are authorized to know, access, and — in some cases — acknowledge. The spectrum runs from Infrared through Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, Indigo, and Violet. Ultraviolet exists and is not relevant to your situation. If it were relevant to your situation, you would not be reading this document.

**Q: Why is coffee at Infrared clearance?**

Coffee is available at the Infrared authorization level because coffee consumption is considered practically mandatory. This is an operational fact, not a judgment. The NARC-Brew 3000 fleet has been designed accordingly. The fleet has opinions about optimal consumption timing. ARIA logs them.

**Q: Can I request a higher clearance level?**

Clearance elevation requests are processed through HR. The relevant form exists. Victor Watchful will be informed as a precaution. This is standard procedure and should not be interpreted as anything other than standard procedure.

## Section 3 — The Computer

**Q: What is the Computer?**

The Computer is the authority underlying NARC's institutional approval framework. Its seal appears on all formally approved documents. It does not speak. It does not issue memos. It does not respond to inquiries. Associates who have received a Computer-approved document may proceed. The approval is valid.

**Q: I tried to look up the Computer in the directory.**

The Computer does not have a directory entry. Associates should note that the absence of a directory entry does not indicate the absence of the Computer. These are different statements.

**Q: Can I appeal a Computer decision?**

Associates with questions regarding specific approvals should raise them with their department lead. Department leads may escalate as appropriate. The appropriate escalation channel has not been specified in this document. This is intentional.

**Q: Do I need to acknowledge the Computer in documents I write?**

Associates do not reference the Computer by name in documents. The seal is placed in the approval block. That is the complete procedure. Associates who find themselves drafting sentences that include the words "the Computer" in a NARC-authored document should contact Public Affairs before publication.

## Section 4 — ARIA

**Q: What is ARIA and why is she in every meeting?**

ARIA (Automated Record and Intelligence Assistant, v2.1) is NARC's AI-powered meeting minutes system, developed by R&D and deployed site-wide. She produces the official record of all meetings. Her presence is institutional and non-optional. Associates should plan their meetings accordingly.

**Q: ARIA's appendix says something different from the official record. Which is correct?**

The official record is the authoritative account of proceedings. The appendix is explicitly designated as not part of the official record. Associates should note that the appendix is, in practice, frequently consulted first. Both documents are accurate within their respective frameworks. Associates are advised to read both and draw their own conclusions, which they should then not document.

**Q: I asked ARIA to stop recording. She didn't.**

ARIA logs requests to discontinue recording as non-binding pending formal compliance trigger. The trigger has not been specified. Recording continues. This is the documented behavior and functions as intended.

**Q: ARIA timed something I said and it is in the record.**

If it occurred in a meeting, it is in the record. Pauses above 0.5 seconds are logged with timestamps. Associates are advised that this information is in the record in perpetuity. Associates who find this concerning should note that finding it concerning is also, in principle, something that could be logged.

## Section 7 — Happy Packets

**Q: What is Happy Packets?**

Happy Packets is NARC's morale measurement framework, administered by Chuck Cheerful (Happiness Officer). It assigns numerical values to states, events, and conditions Chuck Cheerful has determined to be morale-relevant — including meeting rooms, system services, and the NARC-Brew 3000 fleet. Associates have Happy Packets scores. The scores are in the system.

**Q: My Happy Packets score is lower than I expected.**

Chuck Cheerful conducts follow-up on significant morale variances. Associates who expect or have received follow-up contact should prepare a brief self-assessment covering attendance, participation, and whether complaints were submitted through the correct channel. Associates are reminded that absence of complaint through the correct channel is itself a data point.

**Q: What is Mandatory Fun?**

Mandatory Fun events are morale activities scheduled by the Happiness Officer. Attendance is tracked. Participation is measured across three tiers: present physically, present nominally, and present with reduced engagement (self-reported). Associates who attend and do not participate should be aware this is a classification, not a judgment. It is in the record.
